You don't need the perfect project already written

From organizational need to Creative Brief.

ROBUST can help structure your need into a clear Creative Brief. Start with questions such as:

Who are you?
What problem are you trying to solve?
What do you need created?
Who is the audience?
What should the work accomplish?
What deliverables are needed?
What constraints exist?
What assets can you provide?
What is the timeline?
What feedback can you provide?
What rights or usage requirements apply?
Is the opportunity educational, competitive, commissioned, or paid?

ROBUST turns those answers into a structured opportunity that participating institutions can evaluate.

Not every project is the same

Choose the engagement model that fits your need.

Classroom Brief

Bring an authentic challenge into a participating course or program. Students respond as part of their formation. Best for concept development, exploration, research, early-stage ideas, educational projects.

Creative Challenge

Invite students or teams to respond to a defined problem. Multiple approaches, multiple ideas, one challenge. Best for innovation, campaign concepts, design challenges, storytelling, creative exploration, competitions.

Capstone Project

Work with advanced students on a substantial project connected to their program. Best for longer engagements, complex problems, interdisciplinary work, advanced creative development.

Commissioned Project

Where institution policy and applicable requirements allow, engage qualified students or teams for defined creative work. Best for specific deliverables, real organizational use, advanced students, portfolio-quality work.

Creative Collaboration

Work alongside students, faculty, mentors, and other partners to develop something together. Best for experimental work, community projects, cultural initiatives, innovation, research-driven creativity.

Competition

Set a challenge and invite eligible students to compete. Recognize outstanding work through awards, prizes, exposure, mentorship, internships, commissions, and other opportunities.

Social Impact Project

Nonprofits, community organizations, schools, public agencies, and mission-driven organizations can bring meaningful creative needs into the ecosystem. Students gain experience. Organizations gain creative support. Communities benefit.

Not the instructor — the real audience

Give feedback that develops the creative.

You don't need to become the instructor. Faculty and program leaders remain responsible for educational formation. Your organization contributes something different: the perspective of the real audience, stakeholder, or client. You can respond to questions such as:

Did the student understand the problem?
Does the concept address the brief?
Does the work communicate effectively?
Is it appropriate for the intended audience?
How well did the team respond to feedback?
Was communication professional?
Were expectations understood?
Was the work delivered appropriately?

Your feedback becomes another layer of formation.
