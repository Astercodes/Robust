Not at the very end

The skilled workforce shouldn't be built without the employers who need it.

Too often, employers enter the education-to-work pipeline at the very end. Students complete training. Institutions award credentials. Then employers are asked to recruit. But employers may still have questions:

Can this person actually perform the work?
Have they worked with relevant tools and equipment?
Do they understand our environment?
Have they demonstrated the competencies we need?
Are they ready for the expectations of a real worksite?
Where are the next generation of skilled workers coming from?

Authentic work situations

Turn real workplace problems into learning experiences.

Give learners something real to solve. Employers can contribute structured industry challenges based on authentic work situations. Examples might include:

Diagnose a simulated equipment failure.
Interpret a technical drawing and identify installation requirements.
Develop a preventive maintenance plan.
Inspect a system and document potential safety issues.
Troubleshoot an electrical control scenario.
Estimate materials for a construction project.
Analyze the cause of repeated equipment downtime.
Develop an installation plan from specifications.

Students gain meaningful practice. Instructors gain industry-connected learning opportunities. Employers get early visibility into how learners think, perform, troubleshoot, communicate, and solve problems.

Richer evidence than a résumé

Recruit from demonstrated capability.

A résumé tells you what someone says they know. ROBUST Trades can help participating employers evaluate richer evidence of learner development. Depending on permissions and program design, a learner profile may include:

Competencies

What has the learner demonstrated?

Proficiency

What level of development has been reached?

Evidence

What projects, tasks, labs, or work products support that claim?

Credentials

What certifications or industry credentials have been earned?

Experience

What apprenticeships, internships, projects, or workplace experiences have been completed?

Verification

Which competencies have been assessed or validated by instructors, employers, mentors, or authorized assessors?

Trade Portfolio

What has the learner actually built, repaired, installed, fabricated, inspected, maintained, or demonstrated?

"Do you have experience?"
