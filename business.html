Specific, not generic

Entrepreneurship shouldn't be one-size-fits-all.

Someone building a fashion brand does not need exactly the same formation as someone building a logistics company. A food entrepreneur needs capabilities a SaaS founder may never need. A construction business, consulting practice, beauty brand, agricultural venture, media company, manufacturing business, healthcare enterprise, and e-commerce store operate in very different worlds. Yet aspiring founders are often given the same generic entrepreneurship curriculum.

ROBUST Business changes that.

You first build the universal foundations every founder needs. Then your formation becomes increasingly specific to:

your industry→your specialty→your business model→your market→your business→you

Know before you build

Know what the business requires before you build it.

Every business demands capabilities from its founder. ROBUST Business helps you identify them.

What you need to know

Industry knowledge, market knowledge, financial knowledge, regulatory knowledge, customer knowledge, operational knowledge and more.

What you need to be able to do

Research, sell, negotiate, price, plan, communicate, analyze, manage, design, source, operate, lead and execute.

What you need to build

Products, systems, processes, relationships, channels, documentation, financial infrastructure, teams and operational capabilities.

What you need access to

Suppliers, technology, facilities, licenses, capital, mentors, talent, partners, customers and professional services.

Then work systematically toward those requirements.

Investigate first

Test ideas before betting everything on them.

Having an idea doesn't mean you immediately need to start a company. ROBUST Business helps you investigate first.

"Is the problem real?"
"Who experiences it?"
"How painful is it?"
"How are people solving it today?"
"Would anyone pay for something better?"
"Can you realistically deliver it?"
"Can the economics work?"
"What assumptions could destroy the business?"

Record the evidence behind your decisions and progressively move from assumption to validation.
